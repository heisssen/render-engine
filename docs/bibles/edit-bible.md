# Switchyard Editing Bible

Version 1.0, 2026-10-15. This governs every long-form Switchyard episode: 20-25 min, faceless, one AI narrator, built in Remotion from animated 1914 staff-style maps, real archival photos, real archival newsreel film, text cards (telegrams, quotes, documents) and one music bed per act.

The bar is Epic History TV, Kings and Generals, OverSimplified, Historia Civilis, LEMMiNO, Historically, IMPERIAL, Fall of Civilizations, The Great War, and Ken Burns-style BBC/PBS archival work.

The bible extends `research/history-youtube-market.md` §6, the house canon, and does not replace it. Where the two differ, the stricter rule wins. The Jev results in §11 are already folded into the rules below; rules backed by Jev are marked **[Jev]**.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Emotion, then story, then rhythm.** Murch ranks what a cut must serve: emotion (about half the weight), story, rhythm, eye-trace, screen plane, 3D space. When rules conflict, give up the lower ones first. A cut that lands the feeling but breaks eye-trace is fine. A cut that is smooth but emotionally dead is not. *(Murch, In the Blink of an Eye; StudioBinder summary.)*
2. **Cut where a thought completes.** Murch's blink idea is that a viewer blinks when they "get" an idea, and a cut works the same way. For us the "thought" is the narrator's phrase. Cut at the phrase end or on its stressed word, never mid-word. Test the cut by re-timing it blind: if you pick a different frame each time, the rhythm isn't found yet. *(Murch; Musicbed; Radiolab "Blink".)*
3. **Rhythm is tension and release.** Pearlman splits rhythm into timing (which frame to cut on), pacing (rate of change over a passage) and trajectory phrasing (shaping the energy of a run of shots). Build each sequence as a phrase: accelerate into a beat, then release with a hold. Never keep one flat tempo for minutes. *(Pearlman, Cutting Rhythms.)*
4. **A still photo is a master shot.** Burns treats every photograph as holding a wide, a medium, a close-up and a reveal. Every move has a chosen first frame and a chosen last frame. A long move earns meaning: Burns' Roebling zoom runs 33 s. Sound makes a still feel alive. *(Ken Burns, CineD interview.)*
5. **Archive is not wallpaper.** Archival film used only to "take us back in time" flattens it. Each piece of archive should prove, contrast with or reframe the line it sits under. *(Sundance doc-editors panel: Mary Lampson, Joseph Krings.)*
6. **Sound leads picture.** Use J-cuts (the next sound arrives before the next picture) and L-cuts (the sound carries over the next picture) at every section change. Split edits hide the cut and pull the viewer forward. *(Split-edit practice: Wikipedia "J cut", Filmdaft; Murch's Apocalypse Now opening.)*
7. **Heavy effects only at peaks.** Retention guides agree on this: plain cuts between moments, a big transition only at a reveal, scene change or emotional peak. Stacked zooms and whooshes tire viewers, and older viewers most of all. *(AIR Media-Tech retention guide; pixflow.)*
8. **Teach the grammar early.** The cold open and Act 1 set the film's language: how maps behave, what red means, what a telegram card looks like. Then keep to it. *(Sundance panel, Chyld King.)*

## 2. Pacing targets (seconds)

"Visual change" means any of these: a cut, a new insert, a map stage that visibly changes (a new arrow, border or label), or a photo move that reaches a new subject. A slow drift on its own does not count.

| Segment | Avg shot / visual change | Hard limits |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open 0:00-0:20 | **2-4 s [Jev]** | First image is the most striking real archival frame or claim. No logo. |
| Cold open 0:20-0:45 (cap 1:15) | 3-5 s (canon) | Central question stated by 0:20. "What we'll uncover" by 0:40. |
| Title sting | ≤ 3 s | On a musical hit, then straight into Act 1. |
| Act bodies | 4-8 s | No static frame > 6 s. One map run may last 10-20 s if it animates, and it must be broken by an archival insert at least every ~12-15 s **[Jev]**. |
| Burst passages (mobilisation, the night of 31 July, battle onset) | 1.5-3 s for 5-10 shots | Once every 2.5-4 min, then return to the body tempo. |
| Breathing holds (a key quote, a reveal, a death) | 6-10 s on one image with a slow move | Max 2 per act. A breath, not a stall. |
| Re-hooks | every 2.5-3.5 min | New question, reversal, primary-source quote or "but nobody expected…". |
| Act breaks | 8-15 s interstitial | 2-3 devices (§6.5). Never a chapter card. |
| Ending (last 60-90 s) | 5-8 s, slowing | Answer the cold-open question explicitly. Final image held 6-8 s, then a 15-20 s end screen. |

Body average target is ~5.5 s per visual, so a 22-min episode has roughly 230-260 visual events. Below 200 means it's too slow. Above 330 means we're cutting for its own sake.

## 3. Shot vocabulary by asset type

### 3.1 Animated map
- **Role:** the spine of the episode. It explains where things are and what moves.
- **Moves:** slow camera drift or push (scale change ≤ 15% per 10 s), zoom between scales (city → front → Europe), arrows drawing on with eased heads, borders re-colouring, date counter in a corner.
- **Holds:** 10-20 s, only while something animates. Open every map run of 15 s or more with a 3-5 s real film shot of the thing the map is about (a troop train, a staff car) **[Jev]**. Cut out to an archival insert every ~12-15 s and come back to the same framing, advanced.
- **Grammar:** red = the switch/alternate path. Grey/cream = what really happened. Keep this for the whole episode.
- **Never:** let a map sit unchanged for more than 4 s. Never split-screen a map against film: it scored last on map stretches **[Jev]**.

### 3.2 Archival film (newsreel)
- **Role:** proof that this happened to real people. It is our strongest asset.
- **Treatment:**
  - Conform silent-era footage (shot at 16-18 fps) to natural speed by frame duplication. No sped-up Chaplin motion, and no AI frame interpolation.
  - A slight scale-up (≤ 110%) is fine to hide frame edges or burned-in captions.
- **Shot length:** 2-6 s. Use the moment of action: a door opening, a train lurching. Cut on movement, entering a shot in motion and leaving before the action resolves.
- **Inserts:** a 1-2 s film flash is allowed inside map runs and bursts.
- **Credit:** a source lower-third (archive + year) the first time each source appears.

### 3.3 Archival photo
- **Role:** faces, places and objects when no film exists.
- **Moves:** every photo moves. Use push-in for emphasis ("this detail matters"), pull-out for context (who else was there), and a pan along a line (a column of men, a railway platform). Pick the first and last frame deliberately. Ease in and out.
- **Durations:** 4-6 s normally. One 8-10 s slow push per act for the emotional peak.
- **Inserts:** to show a detail (a face in the crowd, a placard), cut to a tighter crop of the same photo. Don't zoom 300% in one move.
- **Avoid:** 2.5D parallax cut-outs as the default. They scored below a plain push **[Jev]**. Allow at most one per episode, and only on a photo that truly has depth.

### 3.4 Famous-person footage (Wilhelm II, Moltke, Nicholas II, Grey, Joffre…)
- **Order of preference:** (1) real archival film of the person **[Jev, 12/12 first choices]**; (2) portrait photo with a 3-4 s push, then cut to film of them; (3) portrait photo with a push alone.
- **First appearance:**
  - Film or photo plus a name lower-third (≤ 6 words: "Helmuth von Moltke, Chief of Staff").
  - When the person's quote follows, the quote card comes next, not the portrait held under it.
- **Never** show two static portraits side by side under narration. It scored last **[Jev]**.

### 3.5 Text cards (telegrams, quotes, documents)
- **Role:** primary-source voice.
- **Treatment:**
  - The best treatment puts the key sentence as text over real archival film of the place or people involved **[Jev #1]**.
  - For a document whose look matters, such as a telegram form: type it on line by line, in sync with the narrator. Underline the key phrase in red. Push in slowly. Max 12 s, then cut away **[Jev #2]**.
- **Hold:** max 6 s of a static card. A card held under 20 s of reading came dead last **[Jev]**.
- **Attribution:** sender, recipient and date on every card. Spell the source date out in the script.
- **Paper texture:** a scanned period form or plain cream paper colour. No fake burn marks or coffee stains.

## 4. B-roll rules (what to show when the narration says…)

Default: **literal beats mood.** Literal B-roll ranked first and mood-only B-roll last **[Jev]**. Allow at most one mood shot per ~90 s, and only on a closing line.

| Narration names | Show |
|---|---|
| A person | Film of that person, or their portrait. On the first mention add a lower-third. On later mentions use film or photo of them acting (signing, reviewing, travelling). Never a lookalike or "a general". |
| A place | Map push to the place, labelled → archival film or photo of that place in 1914 (station, palace, border post). For a city, use a period postcard or street photo. |
| An event | Film of that event if it exists. Otherwise film of the event's mechanism: a mobilisation poster going up, trains loading. Then the map stage that records it. |
| An abstract idea (honour, fear, "the plan", the cult of the offensive) | The concrete object that carried the idea: the Schlieffen memorandum page, a war-game map, a speech text card. If none exists, use one restrained mood shot (an empty platform). |
| Numbers | Animated counter or proportional icons on the map **[Jev #2 for numbers]**, then the real object behind the number (the timetable, the train). Round the number for speech; show the exact figure with its source. One number per screen. |
| A date | Date counter or a stamped date on the next card. Not as a separate black card. |
| A counterfactual ("what if…") | The same map frame re-coloured red, or a real image freeze-framed and two-toned with the red switch line drawn across it. This visual stays reserved for the alternate path. |

Before searching for footage, every script line gets a visual-intent tag: `PERSON` / `PLACE` / `EVENT` / `IDEA` / `NUMBER` / `DATE` / `ALT`. *(Practice from faceless-video B-roll guides; Sundance panel on archive with purpose.)*

## 5. Transitions vocabulary and frequency limits

Plain cuts carry the film. Everything else is seasoning. Limits are per 22-min episode.

| Transition | Use when | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut** | Default, on the narrator's phrase end or stressed word, or on movement inside archival film. | ≥ 85% of all transitions |
| **Dissolve** (12-24 frames at 30 fps) | Time passing (overnight, weeks later), or a photo of a place → the same place on the map. | ≤ 12 |
| **Match cut on shape** | A map border becomes a rail line or trench in a photo; an arrow becomes a train; a map river becomes the real river. This scored first among transitions **[Jev]**. | 3-6, planned in the outline |
| **Push / zoom-through** | A map scale change (Europe → Belgium) and act breaks. | ≤ 8 |
| **Hard cut on a music downbeat** | Burst passages only; `tt sync --longform` snaps these. | Inside bursts only |
| **Freeze-frame + two-tone + red line** | Entering the alternate timeline (the "switch"). | 2-3 (cold open, Act 1→2, maybe climax) |
| **White flash** (4-6 frames) | One shock moment (declaration of war, an assassination). | ≤ 1 |
| **Whip-pan blur** | Not between real footage. At most once, between two map positions far apart. | ≤ 1 |
| **Film-burn light leak** | Never. Light leaks and whips on every cut scored at the bottom **[Jev]**. They read as a template, and the leak is a texture overlay. | 0 |

Never put two non-cut transitions back to back, and never put a fancy transition in the middle of a sentence.

## 6. Sound design

1. **Under maps:**
   - A quiet music bed, plus 1-3 accent sounds per map run at the meaningful moments: a low drum hit when an arrow crosses a border, a telegraph click when the date changes, a rail clank when the trains move **[Jev #1]**.
   - Never a whoosh on every arrow. That scored near last **[Jev]**.
   - Ambience beds (wind, distant rail, crowd) are optional and stay very low (−30 dB or quieter). Alone they ranked low **[Jev]**, which changes canon §6.3's "add ambient beds under maps" from mandatory to optional.
2. **Under archival film:** give silent film a light, believable sound layer (train hiss, crowd murmur, boots), mixed well below the narrator. No comic or modern sounds.
3. **SFX on cuts:** only when the cut is the event (a door slam, a stamp landing on a document, a cannon). No riser or whoosh before ordinary cuts.
4. **Silence after reveals:** drop the music to silence for 0.5-1.5 s after a major reveal or before the act turn. Then either the narrator alone or a new cue. Use this ≤ 6 times per episode. Never leave more than 2 s with no sound and no motion: viewers read that as the end.
5. **Music:**
   - One bed per act. The cue changes at act breaks and at the twist (~50%).
   - Duck the music under narration to about −20 to −25 dB relative to the voice in calm passages. Raise it only in bursts and at act breaks.
   - Bring the new cue in as a J-cut, 1-2 s before the new section's first picture. Let the outgoing cue tail over the first shot as an L-cut, or end it on a downbeat.
6. **Stingers:** one bell or drum stinger per act break (§6.5). Keep a recurring sound motif, such as a single telegraph click or a rail switch clunk, for "the switch" moments.

### 6.5 Act breaks (no chapter cards)
Pick 2-3 of these, ranked by Jev:
1. Map zoom-out to Europe plus a deep stinger, with the arrows flipping red.
2. Freeze-frame on archival film → two-tone → red switch line, with a new cue.
3. Silence for 1 s plus a rolling date counter plus a new cue.

A hard cut with no marker loses viewers, and a chapter card ("PART II") came last **[Jev]**.

## 7. Effects: allowed and forbidden

**Allowed:**
- Photo moves (push, pull, pan) and crop inserts.
- Map animation (draw-on, re-colour, labels, counters).
- Freeze-frame, and two-tone or monochrome tint on real footage for the switch device.
- Gentle vignette on maps only (≤ 15%).
- Light stabilisation, deflicker and exposure balancing on archival film.
- Frame-duplication speed conforming.
- Red underline or highlight on text.

**Forbidden (hard rules):**
- **No film-grain overlays**, no dust or scratch overlays, no light leaks, no fake burn or VHS textures.
- **No AI-generated images or video** of any kind: no generative fill, AI colourisation, AI face restoration, AI upscaling that invents detail, AI frame interpolation, or "reconstructed" photos.
- **Real archival material only.** No re-enactment stock passed off as 1914, and no lookalike photos.
- **No Russian-language anything:**
  - no Russian narration, songs or newsreel audio;
  - no Russian on-screen text, subtitles or map labels;
  - crop or skip footage with Russian intertitles or captions;
  - map labels in English (period name, with the modern name in brackets where useful).
- **No AI avatars or talking heads.**
- **No cheap-template tells:** glitch effects, shakes on maps, emoji, kinetic-type subtitles, karaoke-style captions, spinning 3D logos.

## 8. On-screen text

- **Lower-thirds:** names, roles, places and dates, ≤ 6 words, on screen 3-4 s, on the first mention only.
- **Source line** on every archival item and every claim card, in small type in a corner (e.g. "IWM Q 53446", "British Pathé 1914"). Keep it small but legible.
- **Quotes:** given in full, with speaker and date. If a quote runs over 2 lines, split it across two cards or put it over film. Use English translations; the original German/French text may appear as a faint secondary line.
- **Burned-in subtitles:** none by default. Upload clean captions to YouTube. If we burn them for Shorts cut-downs, use phrase captions, not karaoke.
- **Type and safe area:** keep everything at TV-safe sizes (≥ 36 px at 1080p for body text) inside the 90% safe area. Essential information is never only in small text.
- **Fonts:** one serif or typewriter face for documents and one clean sans for labels. Nothing else.

## 9. Editor-agent checklist (run per episode)

**Structure**
- [ ] Cold open ≤ 1:15. First 5 s is real archival material or the strongest claim. Question stated by 0:20. Sting ≤ 3 s after the cold open.
- [ ] Three continuous acts at ~25/50/25%. Twist at 45-55%. Re-hook every 2.5-3.5 min. No chapter cards.
- [ ] The cold-open question is answered in the last 60-90 s. End screen is 15-20 s.

**Pacing (compute from the timeline)**
- [ ] Shot length in the first 20 s averages 2-4 s; 3-5 s to the end of the cold open; body averages 4-8 s.
- [ ] No static frame > 6 s. No map unchanged > 4 s. No map run > 15 s without an archival insert.
- [ ] Bursts every 2.5-4 min. No more than 2 breathing holds per act.
- [ ] Total visual events in the 200-330 range.

**Assets**
- [ ] Every script line carries a visual-intent tag. B-roll is literal by default, with ≤ 1 mood shot per 90 s.
- [ ] Every named person gets film if it exists, otherwise a moving portrait, plus a first-mention lower-third.
- [ ] Every photo has a deliberate start and end frame. Parallax ≤ 1.
- [ ] Silent film is speed-conformed.
- [ ] Every archival item has a source line, and its source appears in the description credits.

**Transitions**
- [ ] Cuts ≥ 85% of transitions.
- [ ] Dissolves ≤ 12, match cuts 3-6, white flash ≤ 1, whip ≤ 1, light leaks 0.
- [ ] No two non-cut transitions in a row.

**Sound**
- [ ] One cue per act plus a cue change at the twist. J-cut music entries at section changes.
- [ ] 1-3 accent SFX per map run, no SFX on routine cuts.
- [ ] Silence drops ≤ 6 and each ≤ 1.5 s. No 2 s of dead air anywhere.
- [ ] Narrator always intelligible, with music ducked under the voice.

**Hard rules gate (fail = no export)**
- [ ] No grain or overlays.
- [ ] No AI-generated or AI-altered imagery.
- [ ] Real archive only.
- [ ] No Russian-language audio, text or labels.
- [ ] Music cleared for Content ID.
- [ ] Violent archival material passes the ad-safety review.

## 11. Jev results and what they changed

**Method:**
- Script: `tools/edit_bible_jev.py`. Raw output: `runs/edit-bible-jev.json`.
- Each question offered 4-5 alternative edits of the same 15-40 s stretch of *What if Germany attacked Russia first in 1914?*, with the same narration line.
- Six YouTube personas (`niches/_base.yaml` yt_personas) answered.
- Each question was asked twice, once in forward and once in reversed option order, to cancel position bias. Borda points were summed (max 78 for 5 options, 66 for 4).
- **Backend: Jev (typesafe/jev-1.13) answered all 16 rounds**, with no local fallback.

**Caveat:** the helper asks Jev a "would you stop / keep watching" question. It measures pull, not taste, so the results were used to settle choices, not to override the hard rules.
